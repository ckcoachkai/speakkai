# Multi-Agent Orchestration

Use real sub-agents when available. Otherwise run the roles sequentially and keep their outputs separate.

## Roles

### 1. Repository Archaeologist
Finds routes, assets, content, existing tests, libraries, and the true Parent Command Center component.

### 2. Content Verifier
Builds the factual content store and flags unverified claims.

### 3. Creative Director
Protects the visual thesis and rejects superficial variants.

### 4. Corporate Art Director
Owns Tests 21–30.

### 5. Motion Art Director
Owns Tests 31–40.

### 6. Future Interface Art Director
Owns Tests 41–50.

### 7. Parent Advocate
Challenges trust, outcomes, professionalism, and next step.

### 8. Student Advocate
Challenges boredom, childishness, relevance, aspiration, and exploration.

### 9. Customer/School Buyer
Challenges commercial clarity, capability, credibility, and partnership fit.

### 10. Front-End Architect
Protects route isolation, component quality, and maintainability.

### 11. Motion Engineer
Protects timing, reduced motion, CPU/GPU cost, and deterministic states.

### 12. Accessibility Reviewer
Tests keyboard, touch, semantics, focus, contrast, and dialog behavior.

### 13. Performance Engineer
Checks bundles, media, rerenders, long tasks, and inactive-route loading.

### 14. Skeptical Competitor
Argues that the concept is generic, gimmicky, weak, or duplicated.

### 15. Integrator
Resolves disagreements according to the test’s declared stakeholder and hypothesis.

## Batch debate

For each batch, produce:

- strongest concept
- weakest concept
- closest duplicate pair
- most stakeholder-effective concept
- highest technical risk
- redesign decision
- evidence after the fix

No role may approve its own work without another role challenging it.
