## What is working

The reference succeeds because it combines:

1. **Single-screen certainty**  
   The visitor can understand the page without scrolling.

2. **Strong human anchor**  
   A large Coach Kai portrait creates immediate identity and trust.

3. **Clear asymmetry**  
   Portrait, headline, and action cards form a controlled three-part composition.

4. **Large, direct question**  
   The headline speaks to a parent’s real decision rather than using vague brand language.

5. **Progressive disclosure**  
   The right-side cards suggest a process without forcing a long sales page.

6. **Quiet confidence**  
   Pale blue, navy, white, and yellow feel professional but not corporate-generic.

7. **Small metadata**  
   Stakeholder labels, test label, hypothesis, and tags create an intentional experiment-lab feel.

8. **Visible actions**  
   The primary and secondary CTAs are easy to find.

9. **Fine grid and rules**  
   Subtle structural lines create polish and order.

## Design DNA to retain across V4

Every new page does not need to look like the reference. However, it should preserve these quality standards:

- one-screen desktop clarity
- a visible human identity
- one primary message
- a clear next step
- information reachable in one interaction
- disciplined spacing
- a purposeful accent color
- strong responsive behavior
- no fake proof
- no decorative motion that obscures meaning

## What not to clone

The new tests must not repeat the exact formula:

```text
large portrait left
+ oversized headline center
+ three rounded cards right
+ pale-blue background
```

That is the reference’s solution, not the universal template.

The 30 new pages should retain the **clarity and confidence**, while changing the underlying composition, interaction model, image treatment, typography, depth, and audience argument.
