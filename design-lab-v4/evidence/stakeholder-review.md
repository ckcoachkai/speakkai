# V4 stakeholder and adversarial review

Date: 2026-08-31

These are simulated internal reviewer perspectives based on rendered screenshots and interaction checks. They are not interviews, usability studies, or conversion results.

## Tests 21–30 — Corporate, institutional, trust

### Test 21 — Boardroom Editorial

- Parent: Understands that coaching begins with the real speaking moment; still wants learner-specific proof.
- Student: Sees a serious coach but little play or personal exploration; appropriate because Student is not primary.
- Customer: Quickly understands audience, method, and program-conversation CTA.
- Adversarial decision: Retain. Keep the human portrait and concrete service line so it does not become generic consulting.

### Test 22 — Swiss Evidence Grid

- Parent: Can compare task, practice, public evidence, and next step without exaggerated claims.
- Student: The grid is usable but emotionally quiet.
- Customer: Sees disciplined evidence handling, though available proof is visibly limited.
- Adversarial decision: Retain. The honest sparse proof set is more credible than invented testimonials.

### Test 23 — Annual Report Live

- Parent: Understands the preparation sequence and sees professional discipline.
- Student: May perceive the report metaphor as formal or adult.
- Customer: Reads the coaching method as a controlled service process.
- Adversarial decision: Retain. Continue calling it a practice overview, never an impact or financial report.

### Test 24 — Executive Keynote

- Parent: Sees authority and a clear rehearsal process but may assume the service is adult-only.
- Student: Stage scale makes speaking aspirational rather than remedial.
- Customer: Understands presentation and leadership-communication preparation within five seconds.
- Adversarial decision: Shortlist. Keep “coaching preparation” visible to avoid accidental keynote-booking positioning.

### Test 25 — Parent Decision Matrix

- Parent: Immediately recognizes common goals and a practical way to start a fit conversation.
- Student: The page speaks about the student more than to the student.
- Customer: Understands the task-led method but sees this as a family pathway, not a B2B offer.
- Adversarial decision: Shortlist. Preserve “possible starting point” and avoid diagnostic recommendations.

### Test 26 — Institutional Blueprint

- Parent: Sees planning discipline but may find the technical metaphor cold.
- Student: Has little reason to explore; acceptable for a school-first concept.
- Customer: Can map audience, moment, focus, and format without assuming a package.
- Adversarial decision: Shortlist. The plain-language inspector is essential to commercial clarity.

### Test 27 — Coaching Atelier

- Parent: Sees a focused, human, premium private-coaching experience.
- Student: Feels mature rather than childish, though not especially interactive.
- Customer: Understands message, delivery, and refinement but needs format detail in a later production page.
- Adversarial decision: Retain. Do not add scarcity, “elite,” or prestige claims.

### Test 28 — Case Study Wall

- Parent: Understands what practice can look like and trusts the explicit process/proof distinction.
- Student: Can imagine the stages of another attempt but sees limited visual energy.
- Customer: Sees a repeatable method, while real case-study evidence remains Missing.
- Adversarial decision: Shortlist. It is the strongest evidence-minded direction precisely because it does not fake cases.

### Test 29 — Documentary Press Room

- Parent: Can inspect Coach Kai, tools, and public routes, but not learner outcomes.
- Student: The contact-sheet treatment feels contemporary; the evidence set itself is thin.
- Customer: Understands that public context exists but may expect more approved event/workshop media.
- Adversarial decision: Retain with limitation. Production candidacy requires a rights-cleared photo archive.

### Test 30 — Corporate Motion Identity

- Parent: Sees a confident brand and clear service modes, though motion can feel less reassuring.
- Student: Responds to the large type and dynamic portrait treatment.
- Customer: Understands the route from idea to delivery and sees a clear contact action.
- Adversarial decision: Retain. Keep movement finite and secondary to the proposition.

## Tests 31–40 — Fun, motion, kinetic

### Test 31 — Skill Galaxy

- Parent: Sees useful skills but needs reassurance that the galaxy is not a curriculum or tracker.
- Student: Can explore ideas, story, debate, and presence in an aspirational space.
- Customer: The offer is understandable only after reading the persistent service line.
- Adversarial decision: Retain. Increase portrait/supporting trust if developed further.

### Test 32 — Stage Quest

- Parent: Understands practice as manageable steps rather than instant transformation.
- Student: Sees an approachable progression without scores, coins, or rankings.
- Customer: Recognizes a method, but the game path is not suited to every institutional buyer.
- Adversarial decision: Retain. Keep language mature for teenagers.

### Test 33 — Comic Speech Lab

- Parent: Understands story structure and sees real teaching content behind the energy.
- Student: Finds the page memorable, explorable, and less school-like.
- Customer: Can understand the learning model, though the visual tone may not fit formal procurement.
- Adversarial decision: Shortlist. Preserve semantic reading order and one active panel at a time.

### Test 34 — Kinetic Type Jam

- Customer: Understands a differentiated workshop/coaching idea rather than decoration alone.

### Test 36 — Sticker Cutout Studio

### Test 37 — Classroom Desk World

### Test 38 — Rhythm & Voice Visualizer

### Test 39 — Transformation Flipbook

### Test 40 — Motion Poster Festival
