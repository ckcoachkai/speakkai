# Versions 5–8 evolution review

## Checkpoint record

- **5.1–8.1 — structural evolution:** unique information architecture, verified public-experience evidence, audience paths, process, and closing CTA added without sharing CSS with Versions 1–4.
- **5.2–8.2 — first rendered review:** captured at 1920×1080, 1440×900, 1024×768, 768×1024, 390×844, and 360×800. Kept the four distinct art directions and rejected a shared card/hero system.
- **5.3–8.3 — responsive refinement:** increased the Version 6 mobile headline separation after the first 390×844 render showed its summary sitting too tightly under the display type.
- **5.4–8.4 — maturity gate:** 48/48 route/viewport states passed at six target sizes. At this point, further common styling would trade away differentiation rather than create an obvious portfolio-wide improvement.

## Version 5 — Coaching Atelier, Refined

**Strategy:** preserve Version 1's warm editorial character while turning a beautiful introduction into a complete parent/private-client decision journey.

- Senior UI/UX: the hero, verified experience, process, paths, and closing action form a legible narrative.
- Creative direction: the portrait mat, oversized SK monogram, serif typography, and editorial rules keep it bespoke.
- Conversion: a specific speaking-goal CTA appears before and after sufficient context.
- Parent/customer: process and public experience make the offer more inspectable without inventing packages or outcomes.
- Student: quieter than Versions 6–7 by intent; the human portrait and real speaking tasks prevent it from becoming formal or cold.
- Counter-review: the minimalist argued against adding more proof cards; the performance review rejected extra video; the brand review retained the cream/navy restraint.

## Version 6 — Cinematic Stage, Refined

**Strategy:** keep Version 2's dramatic portrait and stage energy, then make the offer, audience paths, evidence, and method readable before spectacle becomes ambiguity.

- Senior UI/UX: chapter cues and clear CTAs make the cinematic composition navigable.
- Creative direction: dark stage lighting, oversized serif type, and an evidence reel remain the strongest emotional direction.
- Conversion: three audience paths translate aspiration into an actionable preparation context.
- Parent/customer: public-record language and visible method reduce the risk of style without substance.
- Student: strongest aspiration and performance energy of the four.
- Counter-review: the accessibility reviewer rejected smaller mobile display type; the conversion reviewer kept two CTAs; the performance reviewer retained CSS-only light motion and no autoplay media.

## Version 7 — Communication Focus System

**Strategy:** retain Version 3's spatial/glass language but replace diffuse floating information with an obvious focus finder and practice loop.

- Senior UI/UX: goal cards answer “what can I work on?” before the practice sequence.
- Creative direction: orbit geometry, portrait arch, and translucent planes keep a distinct spatial identity.
- Conversion: low-pressure “ask about fit” language matches visitors still diagnosing the need.
- Parent/customer: verified-experience planes are visible immediately without unsupported testimonials.
- Student: modern and exploratory without juvenile graphics.
- Counter-review: the minimalist retained open space; the performance engineer kept effects CSS-only; the brand strategist prevented neon or generic glassmorphism from taking over.

## Version 8 — Program Architecture

**Strategy:** evolve Version 4 into the clearest institutional/commercial direction through a program selector, verified scale, and a planning sequence.

- Senior UI/UX: keyboard-operable tabs let buyers inspect paths without a long repeated card wall.
- Creative direction: blueprint grid, technical stamp, and squared program deck keep it distinct from the other three.
- Conversion: “start with a brief” is a credible institutional next step.
- Parent/customer: facts are separated from the explicit boundary that paths are not fixed packages, prices, capacity, or guaranteed outcomes.
- Student: intentionally secondary; the bold typography and selectable deck avoid generic corporate SaaS styling.
- Counter-review: the conversion reviewer retained the inspector; accessibility required click/keyboard equivalents; the strategist rejected invented package tiers.

## Cross-pollination decisions

Shared only where it improved usability: navigation lineage, CTA focus treatment, reduced-motion support, verified-evidence language, and editor hooks. Typography systems, hero compositions, information order, image treatment, section rhythm, and interaction models remain theme-specific.
