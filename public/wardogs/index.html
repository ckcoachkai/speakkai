Helicopter: Raise collective gently. Tilt forward to accelerate; pull back to slow. Bank and use pedals for turns. Momentum persists after releasing controls. Reduce collective for a controlled descent; high sink rates can cause vortex ring state.

Airplane: Increase throttle, accelerate down the runway, then gently hold S above rotation speed. Use bank to turn. Avoid abrupt pitch at low airspeed; reduce angle of attack to recover from a stall.

SCENERY & FLIGHT MODEL

Built from references.

The Shanghai Tower, World Financial Center, Jin Mao Tower and Oriental Pearl use researched proportions aligned with OpenStreetMap coordinates. A roughly 4 km radius core uses selected mapped building footprints, roads and water outlines from the Shanghai Geofabrik extract. Many heights are estimated, mapping coverage is incomplete and geometry is simplified. Outside that core, scenery is procedural. This is not a complete Shanghai 3D model or photogrammetry.

The airport and weapons range are fictional and separate from the landmark district. Targets are practice boards; civilian buildings do not function as combat targets.

Helicopter thrust acts along the rotor axis with rotor spool, collective lag, angular inertia, momentum, ground effect, translational lift and vortex ring behaviour. Fixed wings use air-relative velocity, angle of attack, lift, drag and stalls. Stability assistance can be switched off.

WARDOGS informed the controls and presentation. Its proprietary flight equations are not public; this model is our own approximation. Cockpits and aircraft are modelled interpretations, not manufacturer-exact avionics.

Threat training is optional and limited to the fictional range. Proximity warning tones accelerate as an incoming missile closes. Press R to release a pair every half-second for five seconds, consuming up to 20 flares. The dispenser then cools for two seconds. Flares can distract heat-seeking missiles; they do not guarantee protection.

Inventory pauses flight. Weapon fire is enabled for military aircraft within the practice range. Civilian aircraft remain unarmed.
