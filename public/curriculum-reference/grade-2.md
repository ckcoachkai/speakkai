# Grade 2 — Opinions and Expressive Performance: Proposed Weekly Plan

Fall 2026 | 20 weeks | Target: one-to-two-minute final speech

This is a proposed teaching plan derived from the [Grade 2 curriculum source](../grade-2.md) and informed by the repository's [practical playbook](../../../PLAYBOOK.md) and [speaking-feedback guidance](../../../research/07-speaking-feedback.md). It expands the source phases into teachable weeks; it is not a transcription of an original weekly schedule and does not record historical homework or observed student performance. Prompts are suggestions that the teacher can replace with age-appropriate, class-relevant examples.

## Course map

| Weeks | Proposed phase | Main question | Checkpoint |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1–4 | Opinion foundations | What do I think, and why? | Short supported opinion using reason and example |
| 5–8 | Expressive performance | How can listeners hear and see my meaning? | Expressive 45–75-second talk |
| 9–12 | Growth and child-friendly issues | What is the problem, who is affected, and what could change? | Structured issue-speech sections |
| 13–16 | Power, Pitch, Pace, Pause and interaction | How do delivery choices and recovery help the audience? | Competition-style rehearsal |
| 17–20 | Integration and showcase | How do I revise and deliver a complete message? | Polished one-to-two-minute speech |

## Adaptable lesson framework

The source does not specify a class length. The sequence below is a proposed, adaptable 120-minute framework; shorten or extend each block to fit the actual timetable and learner needs. Keep the feedback loop intact even when time is limited.

1. **Reconnect and target (10 min):** retrieve the previous skill, name today's audience and purpose, and define one observable success criterion.
2. **Model and notice (15 min):** teacher or student model; listeners identify the target with a simple evidence checklist.
3. **Teach and guided practice (25 min):** unpack the skill, rehearse with a partner, cue card, sentence stem, or visual.
4. **Break / reset (10 min).**
5. **Independent attempt (25 min):** each student gives the day's speech section or complete talk.
6. **Feedback and retry (25 min):** partner/teacher gives one evidence-based observation and one next action; student revises and performs again.
7. **Exit record (10 min):** student records the message, target, and next practice step; teacher notes the next comparable check.

## Phase 1 — Opinion, reason, example, fact versus opinion (Weeks 1–4)

### Week 1 — Say what you think

- **Objective:** State a clear opinion about a familiar choice and make the listener understand the topic.
- **Materials / prompt:** Two-choice picture cards (for example, reading or gaming after school), opinion line, and the stem “I think ___.”
- **Lesson sequence:** Model two answers, one vague and one with a named choice; students sort sample statements by topic, then build an opinion with a partner; each gives a 15–20-second answer; listener repeats the opinion heard, teacher gives one clarity cue, and the speaker retries with the topic named first.
- **Speaking output:** One 15–20-second opinion statement.
- **Proposed homework:** Choose one child-friendly either/or question, write the choice in one sentence, and record or tell the answer twice with “I think ___.” Bring the sentence or a small drawing as a cue.
- **Feedback checkpoint:** Can a listener identify the student's choice after one hearing without a follow-up question?
- **Support / extension:** Offer picture choices and a sentence stem; extend by adding a contrasting “I would choose ___ rather than ___.”

### Week 2 — Add a reason

- **Objective:** Connect an opinion to one relevant reason using “because.”
- **Materials / prompt:** Reason-matching cards and prompts such as “Should a class have a five-minute movement break?”
- **Lesson sequence:** Model an opinion with and without a reason; pairs match opinions to plausible reasons and explain the match; students deliver a 20–25-second “I think ___ because ___” response; partner points to the reason heard, teacher checks the link, and students reorder or replace the reason before a second attempt.
- **Speaking output:** One opinion plus one reason, 20–25 seconds.
- **Proposed homework:** Write one opinion-and-reason sentence about a familiar school or home choice; rehearse it aloud three times, pausing before “because,” and ask a listener to name the reason.
- **Feedback checkpoint:** Does the reason answer “why do you think that?” rather than merely repeat the opinion?
- **Support / extension:** Provide reason banks and oral rehearsal before writing; extend by offering two different reasons and choosing the stronger one.

### Week 3 — Make the reason concrete with an example

- **Objective:** Support an opinion with a specific example that shows the reason.
- **Materials / prompt:** Three-box card: Opinion / Because / For example; scenarios about sharing, fairness, or choosing a class rule.
- **Lesson sequence:** Compare a general example (“it helps”) with a concrete one (“last Tuesday, we shared the markers”); teacher jointly fills the three boxes; students practise with a partner, then deliver a 30–35-second response; listener says which detail made the reason believable, teacher prompts for missing specificity, and speaker adds one detail and retries.
- **Speaking output:** Opinion, reason, and one concrete example, 30–35 seconds.
- **Proposed homework:** Fill the three-box card for one new prompt and rehearse a 30-second answer. Underline the concrete detail and bring the card for a partner to identify.
- **Feedback checkpoint:** Does the example show how the reason matters, with a person, action, place, or result?
- **Support / extension:** Let students draw the example or use a supplied scenario; extend by explaining the result of the example in one more sentence.

### Week 4 — Fact or opinion, then a supported mini-talk

- **Objective:** Distinguish a checkable fact from an opinion and use both accurately in a short supported response.
- **Materials / prompt:** Statement cards about school routines, animals, and hobbies; three-box planning card; simple “fact / opinion” signs.
- **Lesson sequence:** Model how “Can we check it?” helps classify a statement; groups sort and justify cards, then choose one fact that supports an opinion; students plan and give a 35–45-second mini-talk; listeners label the fact, reason, and opinion, teacher gives one content or organization cue, and students clarify the classification and retry.
- **Speaking output:** 35–45-second supported opinion mini-talk.
- **Proposed homework:** Select one fact and one opinion on a child-friendly topic, mark them F/O, and deliver a 40-second talk using one reason and example. Ask a family listener which sentence was the fact.
- **Feedback checkpoint:** Are fact, opinion, reason, and example distinct and in an order the listener can follow?
- **Support / extension:** Use color-coded cards and allow a partner to read the fact; extend by explaining why the chosen fact supports the opinion.

## Phase 2 — Diction, projection, eye contact, expression, posture (Weeks 5–8)

### Week 5 — Diction that carries meaning

- **Objective:** Articulate key words and endings clearly enough for listeners to follow the message.
- **Materials / prompt:** Short opinion paragraph, keyword cards, consonant-ending and multisyllabic word list, listener clarity checklist.
- **Lesson sequence:** Model the paragraph once naturally and once with deliberate phrasing; students mark three keywords and practise them in short phrases; each gives a 45-second opinion talk from a cue card; listener circles any word needing a repeat, teacher models a repair strategy, and student slows only the marked phrase and retries.
- **Speaking output:** 45–60-second talk with three clearly articulated keywords.
- **Proposed homework:** Mark three important words in the Week 4 talk, practise each in a phrase, then record one 45–60-second take. Listen once and note one word to make clearer next time.
- **Feedback checkpoint:** Can the listener accurately repeat the three important words and the central opinion?
- **Support / extension:** Permit a picture or phonetic cue and one extra rehearsal with the teacher; extend by practising a challenging term in a complete sentence without flattening the message.

### Week 6 — Projection and useful orientation

- **Objective:** Use an audible, supported voice and an audience orientation that makes the message accessible.
- **Materials / prompt:** Three listener distances (near, middle, far), room-volume scale, optional audio recorder, visual-focus choices.
- **Lesson sequence:** Teacher models under-projecting and projecting from breath support without shouting; students test a sentence for a partner at each distance, then deliver their talk to a small group; listener reports the first and last words heard, teacher gives one volume/orientation cue, and student changes the cue and retries.
- **Speaking output:** 45–60-second talk audible to the intended small audience.
- **Proposed homework:** Give the talk once to a listener two to three metres away or make a consented private recording; check whether the opening, keywords, and ending are audible, then make one revised take.
- **Feedback checkpoint:** Is the whole message audible and directed toward the listening setting, without treating a particular gaze pattern as the goal?
- **Support / extension:** Start with audio-only or a trusted listener; extend by adapting volume and orientation when changing from one listener to a group.

### Week 7 — Expression, face, and emotional clarity

- **Objective:** Choose facial expression and vocal emphasis that clarify the feeling or importance of the message.
- **Materials / prompt:** Emotion cards (curious, concerned, excited, thoughtful), same short talk in neutral and expressive versions, mute-video or mirror option.
- **Lesson sequence:** Model how expression changes meaning while words stay the same; students identify the intended feeling and mark one sentence for emphasis; pairs rehearse, then give a 60-second talk; listener names the feeling and evidence heard/seen, teacher gives one specific adjustment, and speaker retries that sentence in context.
- **Speaking output:** 60-second expressive talk with one intentional emotional shift.
- **Proposed homework:** Choose one intended feeling for the talk, mark one keyword and one pause, and rehearse two takes. Ask a listener what feeling they received before explaining the intended choice.
- **Feedback checkpoint:** Does expression support the words and purpose, rather than becoming a distracting performance layer?
- **Support / extension:** Offer emotion icons and audio-only expression practice; extend by changing expression between opening and conclusion to show a purposeful shift.

### Week 8 — Posture and a complete expressive talk

- **Objective:** Combine diction, projection, orientation, expression, and posture in a coherent 45–75-second performance.
- **Materials / prompt:** Personal checklist with five skills, cue card limited to keywords, optional consented recording, audience of two or three.
- **Lesson sequence:** Model a complete talk and have students notice one physical choice that helps meaning; students rehearse with a partner, then perform 45–75 seconds; partner records one clear effect and one changeable detail, teacher prioritizes one target, and each student revises and performs a second take.
- **Speaking output:** 45–75-second expressive opinion or personal-growth talk.
- **Proposed homework:** Prepare a final 60–75-second take using no more than five cue words. Rehearse once for content and once for delivery; bring the cue card and write which single delivery choice helped the listener most.
- **Feedback checkpoint:** Can the student sustain an understandable message while using at least one deliberate vocal and one deliberate physical choice?
- **Support / extension:** Use a shorter 45-second version, seated delivery, or a private audio take; extend by adapting one delivery choice after listener feedback.

## Phase 3 — Personal growth and child-friendly issues (Weeks 9–12)

### Week 9 — A personal growth story with a clear change

- **Objective:** Describe a familiar challenge, what the student did, and what changed, while protecting privacy.
- **Materials / prompt:** Story path: Before / Problem or challenge / Action / After; fictional alternatives such as learning a skill, helping a team, or fixing a mistake.
- **Lesson sequence:** Model a fictional growth story with a clear before-and-after; students sequence event cards and choose a safe personal or fictional example; pairs tell 45–60 seconds, listener identifies the change, teacher gives one sequencing cue, and speaker moves the change statement to the close and retries.
- **Speaking output:** 45–60-second growth story.
- **Proposed homework:** Complete the four-part story path with a personal, observed, or fictional example; rehearse a 60-second version and underline the sentence showing what changed. Sharing private or distressing details is optional.
- **Feedback checkpoint:** Can the listener explain the change without guessing what the speaker learned?
- **Support / extension:** Use fictional characters, drawings, or oral planning; extend by adding one reason the action helped.

### Week 10 — Different perspectives and feelings

- **Objective:** Represent two perspectives or feelings fairly in a familiar child-friendly situation.
- **Materials / prompt:** Scenario cards about being left out, losing a game, sharing, or apologizing; perspective chart: “I feel / They may feel / Why.”
- **Lesson sequence:** Model a scenario with two accurate perspectives instead of labeling one person; groups role-play and fill the chart; each student gives a 60-second explanation with one perspective shift, partner paraphrases both sides, teacher corrects a missing reason or unfair assumption, and speaker revises and retries.
- **Speaking output:** 60-second perspective talk.
- **Proposed homework:** Choose a scenario, complete two perspective boxes and one “because” sentence for each, then rehearse a 60–75-second talk. Ask a listener whether both perspectives were understandable.
- **Feedback checkpoint:** Does the talk explain how each person might feel and why, without claiming to know an individual's private thoughts as fact?
- **Support / extension:** Use role cards and sentence stems; extend by identifying one shared need or value beneath the two perspectives.

### Week 11 — Consequences and a possible action

- **Objective:** Explain one consequence of a child-friendly problem and propose a realistic action.
- **Materials / prompt:** Problem–consequence–action organizer; situations involving group influence, honesty, rules, or responsibility.
- **Lesson sequence:** Model the difference between an immediate consequence and a broad judgment; teams map “if / then” chains, then students select one action a child could actually take; each delivers 75–90 seconds, listener asks “What happens next?” or “What could I do?”, teacher targets unsupported consequence or unrealistic action, and student adds evidence or narrows the action before retrying.
- **Speaking output:** 75–90-second problem-and-action section.
- **Proposed homework:** Complete one organizer with a problem, one consequence, and one realistic action; rehearse a 75–90-second answer and prepare a one-sentence response to “Why would that help?”
- **Feedback checkpoint:** Is the consequence connected to the problem and is the proposed action concrete enough to imagine doing?
- **Support / extension:** Provide cause-and-effect arrows and a choice of actions; extend by acknowledging one limitation or possible unintended consequence.

### Week 12 — Message and structured issue speech

- **Objective:** Combine problem, perspective, consequence, action, and message into a listener-friendly issue speech.
- **Materials / prompt:** Five-part organizer, transition bank (“because,” “however,” “so,” “this matters because”), audience cards (classmate, younger student, family).
- **Lesson sequence:** Model a 90-second talk and listeners label each section; students plan for a named audience, rehearse in pairs, and perform 90 seconds to two minutes; partner states the message and one unclear link, teacher gives one organization or audience cue, and student inserts a transition/explanation and retries the unclear section.
- **Speaking output:** 90-second-to-two-minute structured issue speech.
- **Proposed homework:** Draft a complete 90-second-to-two-minute issue speech with five labeled sections, reduce it to keyword notes, and rehearse once while checking that the closing states the message.
- **Feedback checkpoint:** Can the audience name the problem, why it matters, a possible action, and the final message in the intended order?
- **Support / extension:** Use four sections or a partner-held organizer; extend by adapting the example and vocabulary for a second audience.

## Phase 4 — Power, Pitch, Pace, Pause; questions, emotional clarity, recovery (Weeks 13–16)

### Week 13 — Power and Pitch for emphasis

- **Objective:** Use vocal power and pitch changes to signal key ideas without shouting or making every word equally strong.
- **Materials / prompt:** Week 12 excerpt, highlighter for key words, power/pitch listening grid, three audience distances.
- **Lesson sequence:** Teacher models flat, overdone, and purposeful emphasis; students mark one key word per section and practise a breath-supported phrase; each delivers 90 seconds, listener points to the emphasis heard, teacher gives one adjustment, and student repeats the paragraph with a controlled power or pitch change.
- **Speaking output:** 90-second issue-speech excerpt with purposeful emphasis.
- **Proposed homework:** Mark three key words in the issue speech, practise each sentence with one intentional power or pitch choice, and record one private revised take. Note which word should stand out to the listener.
- **Feedback checkpoint:** Do vocal changes make the hierarchy of ideas easier to hear?
- **Support / extension:** Use humming, tapping, or a visual high/low line; extend by varying emphasis between an opening, evidence, and conclusion.

### Week 14 — Pace and Pause for structure

- **Objective:** Control pace and pauses so listeners can process sections, evidence, and the final message.
- **Materials / prompt:** Marked script or keyword outline, metronome-free pacing cards, pause slash notation, timer.
- **Lesson sequence:** Model rushed, over-slow, and purposeful pacing; students mark pauses after the opening, before an important reason, and before the close; pairs practise one paragraph, then perform a timed 90-second-to-two-minute version; listener identifies the clearest pause and a crowded phrase, teacher gives one pacing cue, and speaker retries that phrase and transition.
- **Speaking output:** 90-second-to-two-minute talk with three functional pauses.
- **Proposed homework:** Add three pause marks and one phrase to slow in the speech notes; rehearse twice, once watching the timer and once listening for meaning, then keep the clearer take.
- **Feedback checkpoint:** Do pauses separate ideas and give important words room, while the total time remains within the task range?
- **Support / extension:** Use a shorter excerpt and teacher-timed breathing cues; extend by varying pace between a story moment, explanation, and call to action.

### Week 15 — Audience questions and recovery

- **Objective:** Listen to a question, answer its actual point, and recover calmly when a word, line, or place in the speech is lost.
- **Materials / prompt:** Question cards based on class topics, recovery stems (“Let me say that again,” “The key point is…,” “I need a moment to think”), keyword outline.
- **Lesson sequence:** Model acknowledging, answering, and returning to the message; pairs practise question stems and intentional pause/restart drills; each gives a 90-second talk followed by one question and one planned interruption card; listener checks relevance, teacher identifies one recovery behavior, and student answers again using a shorter, clearer route.
- **Speaking output:** 90-second-to-two-minute talk plus one relevant answer.
- **Proposed homework:** Prepare three likely questions and one-sentence answers; practise the talk once with a planned recovery phrase and ask a listener to choose one question without coaching the answer.
- **Feedback checkpoint:** Does the student remain connected to the question and recover by communicating the message rather than apologizing repeatedly or abandoning the talk?
- **Support / extension:** Allow the student to read the question and use a visible recovery card; extend with a follow-up question or a respectful “I do not know yet” answer.

### Week 16 — Delivery circuit and competition-style checkpoint

- **Objective:** Integrate Power, Pitch, Pace, Pause, expression, audience orientation, and recovery in a timed rehearsal.
- **Materials / prompt:** One-page feedback card with separate content, delivery, interaction, and preparation rows; timer; optional consented audio/video; quiet rehearsal station.
- **Lesson sequence:** Teacher models how to choose one target instead of fixing everything at once; students complete a content station, delivery station, and question station, then perform a full 90-second-to-two-minute talk; partner records one observable effect and one next action, teacher confirms one priority, and student makes a targeted edit and performs the affected section again.
- **Speaking output:** Timed 90-second-to-two-minute checkpoint with one question/recovery response.
- **Proposed homework:** Revise the speech around the Week 16 priority, reduce notes to keywords, and complete two short rehearsals: one for message/structure and one for delivery. Bring the revised notes.
- **Feedback checkpoint:** Can the student identify the target, apply it in a retry, and stay within the intended time?
- **Support / extension:** Use a seated or audio checkpoint and a reduced audience; extend by explaining why a selected delivery choice fits the intended audience.

## Phase 5 — Integration, revision, simulation, showcase (Weeks 17–20)

### Week 17 — Choose the strongest message and revise

- **Objective:** Select or refine one complete topic so the speech has a clear message, purposeful examples, and a suitable audience.
- **Materials / prompt:** Topic/message cards, five-part organizer, audience cards, previous checkpoint notes, revision checklist.
- **Lesson sequence:** Model removing a memorable but irrelevant detail; students state three possible messages and test each with a partner, then choose one and map the complete speech; each performs the opening and close, partner states the message heard, teacher gives one selection/structure cue, and student rewrites or reorders a section and retries.
- **Speaking output:** Revised opening plus close, 30–45 seconds total.
- **Proposed homework:** Produce a complete draft of the one-to-two-minute speech, label its message and two or three supporting sections, and rehearse from keyword notes once without reading sentences.
- **Feedback checkpoint:** Can the listener state the message and explain how each selected example serves it?
- **Support / extension:** Offer a narrowed prompt and organizer with sentence stems; extend by cutting one weak example and strengthening the explanation of the best one.

### Week 18 — Full draft, timing, and audience simulation

- **Objective:** Deliver a complete draft within one-to-two minutes while keeping content and delivery connected.
- **Materials / prompt:** Final draft or keyword notes, timer, small audience roles, feedback card with only three targets.
- **Lesson sequence:** Teacher models a timed run and demonstrates how to mark a cut; students rehearse in trios (speaker, listener, timer), then give the full draft; listener reports message and one delivery effect, timer reports actual duration, teacher selects one high-impact revision, and speaker cuts/adds or changes a transition before a second timed run.
- **Speaking output:** Complete one-to-two-minute draft, twice if possible.
- **Proposed homework:** Make the agreed content or timing revision, prepare a clean keyword card, and complete two timed rehearsals. Record the duration and one sentence explaining what changed.
- **Feedback checkpoint:** Does the draft fit the time while preserving a clear opening, connected middle, and purposeful close?
- **Support / extension:** Rehearse a 60–75-second core version before expanding; extend by adapting one example or explanation to a different audience.

### Week 19 — Final polish and recovery rehearsal

- **Objective:** Apply one final content revision and one delivery priority while practising recovery from a likely disruption.
- **Materials / prompt:** Clean cue card, final checklist, question cards, recovery stems, optional private recording with consent.
- **Lesson sequence:** Students identify their two priorities and perform the complete speech; partner introduces one realistic disruption (lost place, unclear question, or timer cue), teacher gives evidence-based feedback tied to the priorities, and student retries the affected section plus the close without restarting the entire speech.
- **Speaking output:** Final rehearsal, one-to-two minutes, plus recovery or question response.
- **Proposed homework:** Rehearse the final speech twice from keywords, then once for a trusted listener; practise one recovery line and bring only the agreed cue card/materials for the showcase.
- **Feedback checkpoint:** Does the student maintain message, timing, and audience connection after a small disruption?
- **Support / extension:** Permit a quiet first run, trusted audience, or audio submission route; extend by answering an unplanned but relevant question and returning to the final message.

### Week 20 — Showcase, reflection, and next step

- **Objective:** Present a polished one-to-two-minute speech and identify evidence of growth plus one next speaking target.
- **Materials / prompt:** Showcase order, timer, audience listening card, student reflection: “My message / A choice that helped / My next step.”
- **Lesson sequence:** Teacher restates the audience and listening norms, students complete a quiet warm-up and deliver the final speech, audience records the message rather than ranking personalities, and teacher gives one specific evidence-based observation; each student may retry a selected line or answer one question, then completes the reflection and compares it with the Week 1 baseline.
- **Speaking output:** Polished one-to-two-minute final speech and short reflection.
- **Proposed homework:** Share the final message with a family listener or keep a private reflection, naming the speech's message, one delivery choice, and one next-step skill. No new speech is required unless the teacher assigns a revision.
- **Feedback checkpoint:** Is the final speech understandable, organized, appropriately delivered, prepared, and within the one-to-two-minute target; can the student describe the next step using evidence?
- **Support / extension:** Offer a smaller audience, seated delivery, audio/video alternative with consent, or a second attempt; extend by explaining how the speech was adapted for its audience and answering one follow-up question.
