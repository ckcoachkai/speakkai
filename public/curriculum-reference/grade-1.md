# Grade 1 — Little Speakers Adventure: Proposed Weekly Sequence

Fall 2026 | 20 weeks | Target: approximately one minute

This is a **proposed teaching sequence** that expands the source curriculum into weekly lessons. It is a planning aid, not a transcription of the source images and not a record of historical homework or observed student performance. The source does not specify a class length, so the framework below is an adaptable 120-minute session model; shorten, combine, or extend activities for the actual class.

## Course map

| Weeks | Source phase | Proposed arc | Evidence of progress |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1–4 | Stage comfort and daily-life sharing | Safe routines, audible complete ideas, listening, sequence | 5–20-second responses; Week 1 baseline |
| 5–8 | Character, setting, problem, solution | Build a short story one story part at a time | Guided story with picture or prop |
| 9–12 | Fact, opinion, reason, example, message | Turn an idea into a clear mini-speech | 30–45-second mini-speech; Week 12 midpoint |
| 13–16 | Dramatic voice, expression, gesture, dialogue, action | Make a story meaningful and expressive | Expressive story performance |
| 17–20 | Integration, rehearsal, reflection, showcase | Choose, shape, rehearse and share an approximately one-minute speech | Final recording or supportive showcase; Week 20 evidence |

## Adaptable 120-minute session framework

The timings are proposed planning anchors, not requirements: welcome and access check (10 minutes); teacher model and name the target (15); guided language or story construction (20); break and reset (10); partner or small-group rehearsal (20); individual or paired attempts (20); one-target feedback and retry (20); reflection and homework check (5). A learner may use a picture sequence, keywords, audio rehearsal, a trusted partner, or another agreed access route. The communication target stays the same while the route can vary.

## Weekly plans

### Week 1 — Welcome to the speaking space

- **Objective:** Establish a predictable speaking routine and collect a gentle baseline: say a name and one complete idea in an audible voice.
- **Materials / prompt:** Name cards, a speaking object, picture choices, and the prompt “My name is ____. I like/notice __.” Offer an object, place, food, animal, or imaginary choice rather than requiring a family disclosure.
- **Lesson sequence:** Welcome and demonstrate listening posture and turn-taking; model a 5-second answer; rehearse with a partner and a visual sentence frame; invite short attempts; give one observable cue (“face the listener or camera and finish the sentence”); retry with the same prompt.
- **Speaking output:** One 5–10-second complete sentence, with an optional second attempt recorded privately for the teacher’s baseline.
- **Proposed homework:** Choose one safe object, animal, food, place, or imaginary character and say one complete sentence about it to a trusted listener twice; bring a drawing or keyword card if useful.
- **Feedback checkpoint:** Can the listener hear the main words and identify the one idea without prompting?
- **Support / extension:** Accept pointing plus a sentence starter, audio-only practice, or a trusted partner; extend ready speakers with “because” and one reason.

### Week 2 — Add one useful detail

- **Objective:** Expand a short answer with one specific detail so the listener can picture or understand the idea.
- **Materials / prompt:** Mystery objects or picture cards; “I chose ____. It is/has/can ____.”
- **Lesson sequence:** Compare a bare answer with a detailed model; sort details by color, shape, action, sound, or use; build an answer with a partner; perform for a small group; cue the speaker to add one detail; retry and listen for the added information.
- **Speaking output:** A 10–15-second description containing a topic and one relevant detail.
- **Proposed homework:** Draw or select one object and practise “This is ____. It has/is/can ____” three times, changing only the detail if desired; bring the keyword or picture.
- **Feedback checkpoint:** Does the added detail help the listener know more about the topic?
- **Support / extension:** Offer two picture-based detail choices and extra wait time; extend by adding a second detail or a describing word.

### Week 3 — Put ideas in order

- **Objective:** Use a simple beginning–middle–end sequence when sharing a familiar activity or imaginary event.
- **Materials / prompt:** Three-step picture sequences; “First ____. Then ____. Finally ____.” Use a morning routine, making something, a game, or an invented adventure.
- **Lesson sequence:** Physically order picture cards; teacher models sequence words and a pause between steps; partners retell from cards; speaker attempts without reading a script; listener points to the matching picture; teacher targets one missing sequence word; speaker retries.
- **Speaking output:** A 15–20-second three-part retell with at least two sequence markers.
- **Proposed homework:** Put three drawings or household-safe pictures in order and tell the sequence aloud twice using first, then, and finally; a caregiver may point to the pictures without supplying words.
- **Feedback checkpoint:** Can the listener follow which event came first, next, and last?
- **Support / extension:** Keep the cards visible and rehearse one step at a time; extend by adding a feeling or reason after the final step.

### Week 4 — Baseline routine and listening response

- **Objective:** Combine an audible complete idea, one detail, and simple sequence while showing that the speaker can listen and respond.
- **Materials / prompt:** Student-selected picture/object bank and listener stems: “I heard __.” and “Can you tell me more about __?”
- **Lesson sequence:** Review the speaking routine; teacher models a 20-second share and a relevant follow-up; students plan with three pictures or keywords; perform in pairs; partner paraphrases one idea; teacher records baseline evidence and gives one target; speaker retries a selected section.
- **Speaking output:** A 15–20-second personal or imaginative share plus one relevant listening response.
- **Proposed homework:** Tell a trusted listener a three-part mini-share twice; ask the listener to say one thing they heard, then add one detail that was missed.
- **Feedback checkpoint:** After the attempt, can the student repeat or clarify the main idea when a listener asks for more?
- **Support / extension:** Let the student answer a choice question or use an audio recording; extend with “I want my listener to remember ____.”

### Week 5 — Meet the character

- **Objective:** Introduce a story character through an observable trait, want, action, or choice.
- **Materials / prompt:** Character picture cards, toy figures, or invented characters; “This is ____. The character wants/can/is ____.”
- **Lesson sequence:** Teacher models introducing a character without a long backstory; students choose or invent a character; complete a simple character map; practise with a partner; perform the introduction; target one clear character clue; retry with a stronger verb or detail.
- **Speaking output:** A 15–20-second character introduction.
- **Proposed homework:** Draw or choose a character and practise three sentences: name, one trait or ability, and one want; rehearse aloud twice.
- **Feedback checkpoint:** Can the listener tell who the character is and what the character wants or does?
- **Support / extension:** Use two-choice prompts and a character picture; extend by adding a distinctive voice or safe prop.

### Week 6 — Set the scene

- **Objective:** Establish where and when a story happens using two setting details that support the action.
- **Materials / prompt:** Place and time cards; “The story happens in/at ____. We can see/hear ____.”
- **Lesson sequence:** Compare a named setting with a pictured setting; model place plus sensory or time detail; groups build a scene from cards; students retell the character in the scene; listener identifies the setting; teacher cues one relevant detail; retry with a clear pause between character and setting.
- **Speaking output:** A 20–25-second character-and-setting opening.
- **Proposed homework:** Make a two-picture scene and tell where and when it is, then name what the character is doing there; practise twice without memorising every word.
- **Feedback checkpoint:** Does the setting make the listener understand where the action begins?
- **Support / extension:** Offer a place/time word bank and allow drawing; extend with weather, sound, or a reason the setting matters.

### Week 7 — Give the character a problem

- **Objective:** Add a clear, child-friendly problem that creates a reason for the story to continue.
- **Materials / prompt:** Problem cards such as a lost map, a blocked path, a broken toy, or a missed bus; “But ____. The problem is ____.” Avoid requiring real distress or family conflict.
- **Lesson sequence:** Teacher acts out a problem and models the transition “But”; students match characters to problems; build a three-picture story; partner asks “What went wrong?”; perform the problem moment; target a clearly stated problem; retry with one action or consequence.
- **Speaking output:** A 25–30-second character–setting–problem story.
- **Proposed homework:** Choose an imaginary or everyday low-stakes problem and tell what happened in three sentences; rehearse once with pictures and once with keywords.
- **Feedback checkpoint:** Can the listener state the problem after hearing it once?
- **Support / extension:** Provide a choice board and sentence frame; extend by explaining how the problem affects the character’s goal.

### Week 8 — Find a solution

- **Objective:** Complete a short guided story with a plausible or imaginative solution and a clear ending.
- **Materials / prompt:** Solution/action cards; “So ____. In the end ____.” Include asking for help, trying another route, repairing, sharing, or magical solutions.
- **Lesson sequence:** Model problem–attempt–solution with picture cards; groups generate two possible solutions; students choose one and retell all four story parts; listener identifies the solution; teacher targets a complete ending; student retries the final two sentences.
- **Speaking output:** A 30–40-second guided story containing character, setting, problem, and solution.
- **Proposed homework:** Tell a four-part imaginary story to a trusted listener using four pictures or four keywords; rehearse twice and bring the plan.
- **Feedback checkpoint:** Does the ending solve or respond to the stated problem?
- **Support / extension:** Keep all four pictures visible or tell the story in two halves; extend with a character feeling and a final message.

### Week 9 — Fact or opinion?

- **Objective:** Distinguish a checkable fact from a personal opinion and use each appropriately in a short share.
- **Materials / prompt:** Simple picture statements such as “A cat has four legs” and “Cats are the best pets”; stems “A fact is ____” and “I think ____.”
- **Lesson sequence:** Sort statements together; teacher models how to explain the difference; pairs justify two sorts; students choose one topic and state one fact plus one opinion; listener labels each; teacher targets the correct stem; student retries with clearer wording.
- **Speaking output:** A 30–35-second fact-and-opinion share, with picture or sentence support as needed.
- **Proposed homework:** Find or draw one checkable fact and one opinion about a real animal, food, game, or object; say both aloud twice and label them. A fictional-world statement should be labeled as part of an invented story rather than a real-world fact.
- **Feedback checkpoint:** Can the listener tell which statement is checkable and which expresses a preference or belief?
- **Support / extension:** Use color cards for fact/opinion and allow a model to copy; extend by explaining why the student has the opinion.

### Week 10 — Add a reason

- **Objective:** Support an opinion with one understandable reason using “I think __ because __.”
- **Materials / prompt:** Preference picture cards and reason bank; “I think ____ because ____.”
- **Lesson sequence:** Teacher models an unsupported opinion and a reasoned one; students match opinions to reasons; pairs complete and rehearse the sentence; listener asks “Why?”; teacher targets a reason that actually connects; student retries with the sentence frame removed if ready.
- **Speaking output:** A 30–35-second opinion share with one connected reason.
- **Proposed homework:** Choose one safe topic and say an opinion plus one reason three times; draw a symbol for the opinion and a symbol for the reason.
- **Feedback checkpoint:** Does the reason answer “why” and connect to the stated opinion?
- **Support / extension:** Offer three reasons to choose from; extend with “For example” and a specific experience or imagined case.

### Week 11 — Make the reason concrete

- **Objective:** Add one example that makes a reason easier for a listener to understand.
- **Materials / prompt:** Example picture cards; frame “I think __ because __. For example, __.”
- **Lesson sequence:** Compare a general reason with a concrete example; co-create one model; students plan opinion–reason–example with three icons; partners identify the example; teacher targets the link between reason and example; speaker retries the middle section.
- **Speaking output:** A 35–40-second mini-speech with opinion, reason, and example.
- **Proposed homework:** Prepare one opinion–reason–example mini-speech using three keywords or drawings; rehearse twice and ask a listener to name the example.
- **Feedback checkpoint:** Does the example show what the reason means rather than introduce an unrelated idea?
- **Support / extension:** Let the student point to an example picture while speaking; extend with a second example only if the first remains clear.

### Week 12 — State the message

- **Objective:** Organize a 30–45-second mini-speech with an opening topic, one supported idea, and a closing message for the listener.
- **Materials / prompt:** Three-part planning strip: “My topic is __; I think __ because __, for example __; the important idea is __.”
- **Lesson sequence:** Teacher models the three beats and a short pause between them; students plan from a choice of everyday or imaginative topics; rehearse with keywords; perform a midpoint mini-speech; listener states the message heard; give one target and retry one section; record midpoint evidence.
- **Speaking output:** A 30–45-second mini-speech with opinion/fact, reason, example, and message.
- **Proposed homework:** Rehearse the same mini-speech once with the plan visible and once using only three keywords; bring the version that makes the message clearest.
- **Feedback checkpoint:** After listening, can a partner repeat the student’s main message and one supporting detail?
- **Support / extension:** Allow a recorded submission or a small audience; extend with a simple transition such as “Another reason” or “That is why.”

### Week 13 — Change the voice for meaning

- **Objective:** Use volume, pace, pitch, and pauses deliberately to show a character, action, or important idea.
- **Materials / prompt:** A short teacher-created story scene, emotion/energy cards, and a “voice dial”; students may choose a neutral narrator voice or character voice.
- **Lesson sequence:** Teacher performs one line three ways and names the effect; students echo safely, then choose one vocal change for a story line; practise in pairs; perform the line in context; target one audible, purposeful change; retry without forcing an accent or imitation.
- **Speaking output:** A 30–40-second story section with one clear vocal contrast and a pause before an important moment.
- **Proposed homework:** Choose two lines from the student’s story and rehearse them with one planned change in pace, pitch, volume, or pause; record audio only if comfortable.
- **Feedback checkpoint:** Does the vocal change help the listener understand the character, action, or important idea?
- **Support / extension:** Mark the pause with a slash and offer audio modelling; extend by combining a vocal change with a transition.

### Week 14 — Show feeling with face and body

- **Objective:** Use facial expression, posture, and one purposeful gesture to support the story’s meaning.
- **Materials / prompt:** Emotion cards, a clear performance area, and a short scene; visual focus may be toward a listener, picture, or camera.
- **Lesson sequence:** Teacher models a gesture that clarifies an action and one that distracts; students freeze-frame emotions; add one gesture to a story sentence; partner identifies the feeling or action; teacher targets one readable choice; student retries the sentence and gesture.
- **Speaking output:** A 35–45-second expressive story section with one purposeful gesture and facial or posture change.
- **Proposed homework:** Mark one gesture and one facial or posture choice in the story plan; rehearse the section twice, keeping the movement small enough that words remain clear.
- **Feedback checkpoint:** Can the listener explain what the gesture or expression added to the words?
- **Support / extension:** Permit seated performance, a prop, or visual focus on a picture; extend with a movement that changes only at a story beat.

### Week 15 — Bring characters to life with dialogue

- **Objective:** Distinguish narrator and character lines using a clear cue such as a pause, body turn, or voice change.
- **Materials / prompt:** Two-character scene cards and dialogue stems; students may use an animal, robot, object, or invented character.
- **Lesson sequence:** Teacher models narrator–character–narrator with labels; pairs mark who speaks on a simple script or picture plan; practise turns and listening; perform the exchange; partner names each speaker; teacher targets one clear switch; retry the dialogue in context.
- **Speaking output:** A 40–50-second story section containing at least two understandable dialogue turns.
- **Proposed homework:** Select or write two short character lines and one narrator line; rehearse the speaker switches twice using keywords rather than memorising every word.
- **Feedback checkpoint:** Can the listener tell when the narration ends and each character begins?
- **Support / extension:** Use color-coded line cards or a partner narrator; extend by adding a purposeful reaction or question.

### Week 16 — Connect action, voice, and story meaning

- **Objective:** Perform an expressive story scene in which action, vocal choice, expression, and gesture support the problem or solution.
- **Materials / prompt:** Student’s four-part story plan, optional safe prop, and a simple audience response card: “I understood __ because __.”
- **Lesson sequence:** Teacher demonstrates selecting only the choices that serve meaning; students highlight the problem or solution beat; rehearse scene in pairs; perform for a small audience; audience identifies the key beat; teacher gives one priority; student retries that beat with a clearer choice.
- **Speaking output:** A 45–60-second expressive story performance.
- **Proposed homework:** Practise the complete story once with the plan and once with three keywords; circle the one vocal, facial, or movement choice that best shows the message.
- **Feedback checkpoint:** Does the performance choice make the important story beat easier to follow?
- **Support / extension:** Offer audio plus a still image, seated narration, or a trusted partner for dialogue; extend by adding a brief message at the close.

### Week 17 — Choose and shape the final speech

- **Objective:** Select a daily-life or imaginative topic and shape it into a one-minute plan with a clear opening, ordered points or story beats, and a close.
- **Materials / prompt:** Topic choice board, four-box story strip or three-beat idea strip, keyword cards, and the planning question “What do I want my listener to remember?”
- **Lesson sequence:** Teacher models choosing a manageable topic; students pitch two choices to a partner; select one without requiring private disclosure; build an opening, two or three details/story beats, and a message; rehearse the plan; teacher checks sequence and scope; retry the opening and close.
- **Speaking output:** A 45–60-second planned outline or partial speech.
- **Proposed homework:** Complete a four-box story plan or three-point mini-speech plan and practise the opening and closing aloud twice; bring keywords or drawings.
- **Feedback checkpoint:** Can the listener identify the topic and what the speaker wants them to remember?
- **Support / extension:** Use a teacher-provided story or shared class topic; extend by adding one audience-friendly transition.

### Week 18 — Rehearse for clarity and timing

- **Objective:** Deliver the planned speech with audible words, simple organization, and a pace that fits approximately one minute.
- **Materials / prompt:** Keyword cards, timer, optional audio recorder, and a listener checklist for topic, order, message, and audibility.
- **Lesson sequence:** Teacher models a keyword rehearsal and a reset after losing a word; students rehearse in pairs; make a first timed attempt; listener reports the message and least-clear point; teacher targets one change; students edit a keyword card and retry.
- **Speaking output:** One first full attempt, approximately 45–60 seconds, with timing treated as a guide rather than a pass/fail cutoff.
- **Proposed homework:** Make one private audio or live rehearsal for a trusted listener; ask “What was my message?” and “Which part was hardest to follow?” then revise one keyword or sentence.
- **Feedback checkpoint:** After one rehearsal, can the student use the chosen cue card to restore the sequence or continue after a forgotten word?
- **Support / extension:** Use a shorter target, picture cards, or a partner prompt; extend by reducing the script to three or four keywords.

### Week 19 — Full rehearsal and useful retry

- **Objective:** Complete a supportive final rehearsal, apply one feedback target, and practise recovery after a pause or mistake.
- **Materials / prompt:** Final plan, timer, optional prop, and recovery stems “Let me say that again,” “The important part is ____,” or a silent breath and glance at a keyword.
- **Lesson sequence:** Warm up voice and sequence; perform a full rehearsal for a small audience or teacher; audience gives the message heard and one observable delivery note; teacher selects one priority; student retries the unclear section or whole speech; compare what changed.
- **Speaking output:** Approximately one-minute full rehearsal, followed by a targeted retry.
- **Proposed homework:** Rehearse the final speech once with a trusted listener and once independently; practise one recovery stem and bring the final keyword card or drawing.
- **Feedback checkpoint:** Does the student use one recovery or feedback action and keep the message understandable?
- **Support / extension:** Present first to the teacher, submit audio, or use a partner narrator; extend by answering one friendly audience question about the speech.

### Week 20 — Supportive showcase and reflection

- **Objective:** Present an approximately one-minute daily-life or imaginative speech, listen respectfully to others, and identify one next speaking goal.
- **Materials / prompt:** Final keyword card or picture plan, optional prop, private recording only with consent, and reflection stems “I made ____ clear” and “Next time I will ____.”
- **Lesson sequence:** Review the audience and recovery routine; offer a quiet rehearsal; each student presents live to the agreed audience or through an access route; listeners respond with the message they heard; teacher records final evidence on audibility, complete ideas, organization, expression, participation, and growth; student retries a short line if desired and completes reflection.
- **Speaking output:** Approximately one-minute final speech or an equivalent supported audio/paired presentation; the target is developmental, not a hard pass/fail cutoff.
- **Proposed homework:** Share the speech, drawing, or audio with a trusted person if the student wants to; tell them one skill that improved and choose one small speaking goal for the next course.
- **Feedback checkpoint:** Can the student communicate a complete, organized idea to the intended listeners and explain one next step from the experience?
- **Support / extension:** Use a smaller audience, audio submission, visual plan, co-presentation, or another documented access route; extend with a short audience question and response.

## Evidence and feedback use

Keep baseline (Week 1), midpoint (Week 12), and final (Week 20) samples comparable enough to show change, while recording the prompt, support, and route used. Feedback should name one observable behavior, its effect on the listener, and one rehearsable next action; the next attempt is part of the learning loop. Track content, organization, delivery, listening, and access separately so accent, visual focus, quietness, or a single nervous performance is not treated as a personality judgment.
