## V07 recommendation: a rotating listener-role practice card

Add one small guided activity that a teacher can run with a group and that a parent or learner can enter quickly from a phone. Use a mobile link or approved QR/WeChat handoff to open the activity; do not infer that any external provider’s app or QR flow is available for SpeakKai.

Suggested sequence:

1. **Teacher frames the room.** Show the purpose, practice language, time, and one participation rule: listen for a speaking choice, not for a winner.
2. **Speaker makes a short attempt.** Give one situation and one clear prompt. The speaker has a bounded time and may use notes.
3. **Listener uses a constrained card.** The listener records three non-ranking observations: “I understood the main point when…”, “I wanted to hear more about…”, and “One question I still have is…”.
4. **Roles rotate.** A second learner speaks while the first listener becomes speaker or questioner. The teacher can model one example before rotation.
5. **Retry with one choice.** The speaker chooses one adjustment—main point first, pause before an example, clearer transition, or purposeful ending—and tries again.
6. **Private recap.** The learner sees the chosen adjustment and next attempt. The parent/teacher view can show participation and the next practice action without a public score, rank, or comparison table.

The listener card turns “peer feedback” into something a child or adult can actually do. It also gives a quiet learner a meaningful role in the same lesson. The teacher remains responsible for the purpose, timing, modelling, safety norms, and correction of misunderstandings.

## Minimum editable content for this activity

Use the V06 lesson contract, with two group-specific additions:

| Field | Example requirement for the editor |
|---|---|
| Language + audience | Practice language; parent/student, school group, or company team; age/grade or adult context; starting situation. |
| Timing | Total and per-turn estimate; teacher framing, speaker attempt, listener note, retry, recap; mark `illustrative` or `confirmed`. |
| Ordered steps | Teacher prompt, speaker action, listener card, rotation, retry, recap. One action per screen. |
| Feedback / next action | One observable speaking choice plus one next attempt; listener prompts stay descriptive rather than evaluative. |
| Source / review status | Source URL/title, scope note, `draft`/`reviewed`/`approved`, reviewer and date. |
| **Group roles** | `teacher`, `speaker`, `listener`, optional `questioner`; define what each role sees and may submit. |
| **Participation safety** | No public leaderboard/ranking; recording off by default; alias or first name only where needed; sharing requires permission; teacher can remove or correct a comment. |

## Quick mobile entry and safe participation

The first mobile view should contain only the activity name, audience, language, estimated time, and `开始练习`. It should not require a long account setup before a learner can understand the task. If contact is needed, a parent or organizer can send age/grade, audience, goal, rough group size, date range, and delivery format through WeChat; no student records, names, or internal documents are needed for the first inquiry.

For a group, the safe default is a private attempt and a structured listener note. Do not expose a public score, “best speaker,” follower count, or rank. A teacher can still give direct coaching and record an internal completion state if the group has agreed to it. Identifiable audio, video, or student stories should stay off public pages unless the appropriate permission has been obtained.

## What the reference gets right and where it stops

EF’s public structure makes teacher-led practice, realistic role-play, group scale, self-study, and reporting discoverable. That supports a SpeakKai experiment centered on roles and practice rather than a passive lesson page. The public source does not establish that its approach improves speaking, does not show how peers listen or give feedback, and does not verify a quick mobile entry path. Those are design opportunities and test questions, not facts imported into SpeakKai.

The current SpeakKai programme remains **Young Competition Speakers, Grades 1–2, Fall 2026, 18 classes**. The illustrative **10-minute speaking starter** may use the sequence above as a standalone activity, but it must remain labelled illustrative and must not become an invented course unit, class count, outcome, or ranking system.
