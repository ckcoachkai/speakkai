# China benchmark research for SpeakKai flagship communication coaching

Access date: 2026-09-07 (Asia/Shanghai). This is a read-only public web pass. I used direct HTTP requests with a normal browser user-agent and did not sign in, publish, follow, message, submit forms, or bypass a challenge. “Observed” means visible in the fetched public page or search result. Marketing claims remain claims by the publisher; they are not independently verified outcomes.

## What the strongest public sites make easy to understand

The clearest pattern is to let a visitor self-identify quickly, see a concrete learning system, inspect credible people or outcomes, and choose a low-friction next step. Each audience needs a distinct journey: a parent needs age/level, safety, teacher quality, practice visibility, and a trial; a school needs programme fit, schedule, delivery model, safeguarding, and evidence; a company needs a use case, workshop or cohort format, facilitator credibility, and an implementation conversation.

## Audience journeys and trust requirements

**Parent.** Start with age/level and the child’s speaking situation. Show the lesson format, coach credentials, an anonymized or consented sample of feedback, what the learner produces after four sessions, and the exact trial/diagnostic steps. A WeChat CTA should carry the selected goal into the inquiry so the parent does not repeat context.

**School or education partner.** Start with audience, class length, group size, delivery mode, timetable flexibility, safeguarding, and teacher-facing materials. Offer a one-page programme brief with learning objectives, sample rubric, logistics, and reporting cadence. A school lead should be able to request a programme conversation without navigating a consumer checkout.

**Company or executive buyer.** Start with the business situation—client pitch, board update, bilingual presentation, interview, or manager communication—then show workshop/cohort format, outputs, facilitator evidence, implementation steps, and a response-time promise. Put company-specific delivery on its own route, following the distinction visible in CEIBS and 时代光华.

Across all three, the evidence hierarchy should be: a real teaching artifact; a clear method/rubric; named facilitator qualifications; consented, specific learner or client evidence; and only then broad scale or brand claims. Keep private student names, schedules, and free-text event details out of public marketing.

## Iterative hypotheses (non-copying experiments)

Each hypothesis should run as a matched test with one primary metric and a fixed observation window. These are ideas derived from the public patterns above, not claims that the channels currently reward them.

1. **Role-first landing:** compare “I am a parent / school / company” routing with a single generic landing page. Primary metric: qualified inquiry rate per visitor; guardrail: wrong-role inquiries.
2. **Problem-first course cards:** compare cards beginning with a situation (“present a clear update in English”) against cards beginning with a course name. Primary metric: course-detail click-through and inquiry completion.
3. **Output preview:** show a short before/after speech clip plus the rubric that produced the feedback. Primary metric: diagnostic completion and trust rating; guardrail: no unsupported improvement percentage.
4. **Level visibility:** show a three-level progression with sample speech outputs, following the clarity of 51Talk’s L0–L6 grouping. Primary metric: parent self-selection accuracy and booked diagnostic rate.
5. **Coach proof card:** compare a generic bio with a card showing specialty, relevant qualification, teaching format, and one consented sample annotation. Primary metric: “I understand why this coach fits me” survey score and inquiry rate.
6. **Trial versus diagnostic wording:** compare “free trial” with “10-minute speaking diagnostic + next-step note”. Primary metric: completed sessions and qualified follow-up; guardrail: no bait-and-switch.
7. **Parent WeChat serial:** publish a weekly question → exercise → annotated example → fit/next step article. Primary metric: article-to-inquiry conversion and repeat visitors; guardrail: private student data stays private.
8. **Xiaohongshu artifact post:** when public account access is available, compare a saveable annotated feedback card with a teacher portrait post. Primary metric: saves per 1,000 impressions and qualified WeChat inquiries, not likes alone.
9. **Douyin opening:** compare a situation-first first three seconds with an abstract definition. Primary metric: three-second hold, completion, saves, and qualified inquiries. Treat Douyin access and counts as pending validation.
10. **Bilibili lesson playlist:** publish original 5–10 minute episodes using “problem → model → demonstration → exercise”, then compare completion and returning viewers with standalone talks. Primary metric: completion and repeat-viewer rate.
11. **Caption/search wording:** compare an exact searchable English phrase plus Chinese context with a broad inspirational title. Primary metric: search-source impressions, saves, and inquiries; guardrail: title accurately matches the lesson.
12. **School brief CTA:** compare “contact us” with “request the school programme brief” containing class size, logistics, outcomes, safeguarding, and a sample rubric. Primary metric: qualified school leads and response-to-meeting rate.
13. **Corporate use-case route:** compare one generic corporate page with four use-case pages for executive update, client pitch, bilingual delivery, and manager communication. Primary metric: buyer self-selection and meeting quality.
14. **Transparent offer block:** compare a card with format, duration, cohort size, deliverable, and price range against a card that hides all commercial detail behind contact. Primary metric: detail-to-inquiry conversion; guardrail: lead quality and refund questions.
15. **Evidence ladder:** compare scale-first trust copy with an evidence ladder (artifact → method → credentials → consented case). Primary metric: trust/comprehension survey and inquiry rate.
16. **Cross-channel cutdown:** use one original lesson as a short vertical cutdown, a full Bilibili lesson, and a WeChat article with the rubric. Primary metric: qualified inquiries by source, with the same topic and CTA across variants.
