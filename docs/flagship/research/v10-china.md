## Parent-readable course sequence

The sequence can be shown below the factual block. These are translations of the current English source sequence, not new unit topics:

1. **探索主题或角色** — `从一个想法、问题或表达情境开始，明确谁在表达、谁在倾听，以及听众需要什么。`
2. **搭建表达内容** — `确定一个清晰的重点，加入有用的细节和例子，再整理成听众能够跟上的表达。`
3. **表达与回顾** — `完成一次短演讲，根据反馈选择一个下一次要尝试的调整。`

The page should explain that these three stages describe the learning shape across the six three-class units. Do not name or imply specific unit subjects unless the source data later approves them. ## Fact-parity checks before publishing Chinese

- Keep the programme name, season, grade range, 18-class count, six three-class units, 60–90-second practice speeches, and supported three-minute final showcase together so none is lost in translation.
- Use `三节课单元` for `three-class unit`; do not turn it into `三周单元` or imply a weekly cadence.
- Use `练习演讲` for `practice speeches`; do not call them competition performances or promise a result.
- Use `在支持下完成` for `supported`; do not expand that into a named staffing model, private coaching, or guaranteed outcome.
- Keep “topic or role” as `主题或角色`; do not invent topic names from benchmark sites or from the programme’s separate internal theme/role arrays.
- Keep fees, exact lesson times, availability, and booking status in the direct Kai conversation. The public schedule remains a planning reference.
- The current illustrative 10-minute speaking starter may link from the parent page, but it must be labelled `示例练习` or `练习资源（示例）` and must not be presented as one of the 18 classes.

## What the Chinese page should make easy to scan

The first screen should answer, in this order: who the course is for; when it runs; how many classes; how the six units are shaped; what length of speech the learner practises; what the supported final showcase involves; and how a parent can discuss fit. A small “课程事实” strip can carry the seven fixed facts, while a separate “联系前可以准备” block asks only for grade, goal, event timing, and preferred format.

This structure adapts 51Talk’s visible progression logic without importing its 25-minute lesson length, L0–L6 levels, certificates, foreign-teacher model, registered-user figure, or outcome language. It keeps the current SpeakKai course understandable to Chinese-speaking parents while preserving English/Chinese parity.
