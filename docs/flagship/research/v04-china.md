# V04 China-facing course and service discovery notes

Checked: 2026-09-07 (Asia/Shanghai). Read-only public-page review. These observations describe discoverability and content structure only; they do not claim search ranking, traffic, conversion, or learning outcomes.

## 51Talk: course structure a parent can scan

Sources: [51Talk course system](https://www.51talk.com/global/course) — HTTP 200, title `51Talk在线英语，打下英语语言基础，培养英语沟通交通能力`; [51Talk home](https://www.51talk.com/) — HTTP 200, title `51Talk是全球化的在线青少英语教育品牌`.

### Visible structure

The course page’s public title/description metadata carries the core discovery terms and offer shape: online English, one-to-one foreign-teacher teaching, 25 minutes per lesson, six years of iteration, and a publisher-presented registered-user figure. The page headings then make the course logic scannable:

- `51Talk英语课程体系` and `51Talk在线英语课程` establish the product before detail.
- `1对1纯外教教学，25分钟一节课` gives format and lesson length immediately.
- `科学设计课程体系`, `遵循各年龄发展阶段设计`, and `丰富有趣的画面内容` explain the organizing principles.
- `经典英语课程体系` and `汇集全球师资自主研发核心课程——《经典英语》` name the flagship curriculum.
- Four progression headings state distinct purposes: listening/speaking foundation; natural phonics, pronunciation, and vocabulary; interdisciplinary reading; and broader language use and discussion.
- The page links `关于我们`, `学习理念`, `课程体系`, `全球师资`, and `联系我们`; the header also shows `注册`.

The accompanying descriptive copy explains that the curriculum is designed around age development, repeats themes across levels, and links the stated system to CEFR and Cambridge exams. It gives example learning purposes such as simple dialogue, expressing a personal view, reading longer English texts, discussing a question in depth, and writing a clear paragraph. This is a useful content model because it translates a course name into observable activities.

### Discovery opportunities for SpeakKai

1. Use a page title and first paragraph that name the audience, communication situation, delivery format, and next step together. Avoid trying to rank for a broad “English training” category.
2. Give every speaking programme a short sequence of headings: who it suits, lesson format, practice cycle, example output, feedback, and how to discuss fit.
3. Organize levels by what the learner can attempt next—such as one clear point, a short story, a structured argument, or a decision-ready update—rather than by abstract labels alone.
4. Link related pages with descriptive text (`学生演讲辅导`, `学校表达项目`, `企业演讲与沟通培训`) so a visitor and an assistive technology user can predict the destination.
5. Place the current confirmed programme scope in the offer detail: Young Competition Speakers, Grades 1–2, Fall 2026, 18 classes. Keep other ages and formats as discussion paths rather than implying a fixed package.
6. Treat registered-user, teacher, or scale figures as publisher claims and pair them with a concrete practice artifact. Do not add a number unless it is current and approved.

### Corporate discovery structure

The [EF Corporate Learning Chinese page](https://corporatelearning.ef.com/zh/) separates solution families in the primary navigation: AI language learning, immersive online classes, course management, large-organization language assessment, AI solutions, method, business impact, resources, webinars, client stories, and videos. Its descriptive links route to `/zh/solutions/`, `/zh/solutions/program-management/`, `/zh/solutions/assessments/`, `/zh/method/`, `/zh/business-impact/`, `/zh/resources/`, `/zh/contact/`, and named client-story paths.

The page explains a staged corporate service journey:

1. Contact language-learning experts.
2. Analyze business needs through assessment and global survey.
3. Co-design a customized solution.
4. Integrate with an LMS and provide local/global support.
5. Track progress through reports and evaluate the programme.

The public corporate contact page [EF Corporate contact](https://corporatelearning.ef.com/zh/contact/) makes that sequence more operational. Its headings include `联系语言学习专家`, `共同设计`, `持续的学习创新`, and `开展及评估`. The non-submitted form exposes fields for first name, last name, company, work email, phone, job title, and a personal message, plus privacy/marketing consent checkboxes. The page’s description says it is for a solution tailored to the business. No form was completed, and no claim is made about what happens after submission.

### Discovery opportunities for SpeakKai

1. Use the same top-level audience split already present in the flagship copy: students/parents, schools/educators, and companies/event teams.
2. On each route, lead with a plain-language problem and show the service shape immediately: coaching, workshop, programme sequence, keynote, or executive coaching.
3. Give each card descriptive links that say what opens (`了解学生演讲辅导`, `查看学校项目`, `讨论主题演讲或团队培训`) instead of repeating `了解更多` everywhere.
4. Add an “example output” section to every offer. For students this can be a short practice speech and feedback next step; for schools a sample task/rubric; for companies a message outline and rehearsal scope. Use approved, consented material only.
5. Make the corporate journey legible without copying enterprise language: brief → fit conversation → scope and format → agreed materials and confidentiality → delivery → feedback/follow-up.
6. Keep a brochure or programme brief useful on its own. It should state audience, format, duration, group size, learning focus, deliverable, and what remains to be confirmed.
7. Keep “contact”, “request a brief”, and “view the schedule” distinct. The public schedule is a planning reference; it does not reserve a place.

## Content discovery schema for V04

The two refreshed pages suggest a reusable schema for future English/Chinese content without making ranking claims:

| Field | Parent education | School programme | Company/event service |
|---|---|---|---|
| Audience selector | Grade/age and starting point | Age range, language level, timetable | Audience, role, communication situation |
| Offer name | Student coaching or named course | Workshop, lesson sequence, teacher development | Keynote, team workshop, executive coaching |
| Format | Individual/group, lesson length | Session length, group size, delivery mode | Keynote/workshop/coaching, online/in-person/hybrid |
| Practice focus | Organize ideas, story, debate, presentation | Purpose, repeatable task, feedback | Message, structure, delivery, rehearsal, Q&A |
| Observable output | Short speech, story, or next practice action | Task/rubric/reporting agreement | Decision-ready update, presentation rehearsal, agreed follow-up |
| Trust evidence | Coach bio, sample annotation, consented feedback | Facilitator, materials, safeguarding and supervision plan | Relevant experience, case or sample process, confidentiality path |
| Next step | Diagnostic/conversation | Request programme brief | Discuss fit and scope |

Use the same fields in Chinese and English so language switching does not change the factual offer. Keep prices, dates, availability, credentials, outcomes, and proof synchronized with approved source data.
