# V5 global benchmark: usable speaking practice and lead gating

**Access date:** 2026-09-07 (Asia/Shanghai)  
**Source reviewed:** [Toastmasters International — Public Speaking Tips](https://www.toastmasters.org/resources/public-speaking-tips) and [Preparing a Speech](https://www.toastmasters.org/resources/public-speaking-tips/preparing-a-speech). Both returned HTTP 200.  
**Scope:** One strong official public resource flow, reviewed for immediate usefulness, practice specificity, and whether a visitor must submit contact details before receiving value. No form was submitted and no conversion result is claimed.

## What the visitor can use immediately

The hub promises actionable, step-by-step guidance and links to focused topics such as writing/delivering a speech, overcoming fear, giving a toast, and writing a eulogy. The linked “Preparing a Speech” page gives concrete actions:

- organize the speech as opening, main points, and summary;
- rehearse frequently, using friends or a mirror as the audience;
- use a timer to practise pace;
- become familiar with the venue, stage, obstacles, and entrances/exits;
- choose comfortable, professional clothing; and
- use visual aids to help the audience understand and reinforce the speech.

The page also links to more practice guidance, including “Prep Talk” and a speech-building guide. Its “Find a Toastmasters Club” CTA says visiting a club meeting is free, so the next step is available without first buying a course.

## Lead gating and what works

The checked resource pages are open: the visitor can read the steps without an email form, login, payment, or quiz gate. The conversion path is soft and contextual: after giving practical value, the site offers free club attendance, more tips, and a club finder. This is a useful model for SpeakKai’s first practice interaction: let a parent, teacher, or learner complete one small exercise before asking for contact details.

The practice advice is specific enough to try today, yet short enough to scan. It covers preparation and delivery, rather than only motivational copy. The page also acknowledges real settings and visual aids, which helps a speaker transfer practice to an event.

## Gaps relevant to SpeakKai

The resource is generic. It does not visibly adapt to learner age/level, parent support, a classroom, or a company presentation. It asks the visitor to self-direct rehearsal and does not provide a recording upload, rubric, coach response, progress history, or teacher/parent handoff. The page does not expose a downloadable worksheet in the checked content. The free-club CTA is useful but depends on a recurring local format; an async or private route is not presented on this resource page.

These are observations about the public page, not claims that Toastmasters lacks such features elsewhere. The page also does not establish that open resources convert better than gated resources; it only demonstrates a low-friction value-first pattern.

## Four applicable SpeakKai changes

1. **Put one complete practice card before the inquiry gate.** Include a prompt, target time, simple structure, delivery instruction, and a three-question self-check. Example: “Record 60 seconds; name the idea, give one concrete example, finish with the listener takeaway.” This is a proposed SpeakKai format, not a Toastmasters curriculum claim.

2. **Ship an original printable one-object-story starter.** A learner chooses one everyday object, gives it context, describes one change or tension, and ends with why the listener should care. Label the sheet as an original SpeakKai starter, state the intended age/level, and offer a short model plus a blank version. Do not present it as an established universal method.

3. **Use progressive disclosure for leads.** Keep the first practice and printable resource public. Offer optional email/WeChat delivery, feedback, or a parent/teacher progress copy after the visitor has completed the exercise. Ask for audience, age/level or team size, goal, language, and timing only when those details are needed for a response.

4. **Route the next action by audience and feedback need.** After practice, show separate options: parent consult, teacher/classroom demo, company/team conversation, or self-guided resources. Explain whether the action creates a request or a confirmed booking; any schedule or session must remain pending Kai confirmation.

## Compact implementation test

For each public practice resource, verify that a first-time visitor can find the prompt, understand the time requirement, complete it without an account, and reach a relevant next action. Check mobile readability, keyboard access, English/中文 parity, printable layout, and the absence of private student data. Measure only operational signals such as practice-start rate, completion where instrumented, optional inquiry quality, and support burden; do not claim a conversion lift without a controlled test.
