## What the source establishes

Duarte’s public page says many presentation problems begin earlier with unclear thinking and unfocused messaging: presenters can lead with everything they know instead of what the audience needs, leaving content thorough but ineffective. Its public guidance repeatedly points to audience needs, a clear persuasive message, a defined stake, and helping the audience know what to think, feel, or do differently.

The page also describes audience-first work: identify what the audience cares about, surface needs and tensions, and shape real presentation content into a clear story. These are provider descriptions of a communication approach. They are useful design signals, not independent evidence that a SpeakKai activity improves business decisions or presentation outcomes.

## Strength for V8

The source gives V8 a strong adult/company focus. A workplace speaker needs to compress an idea for a real listener, explain why the idea matters to that listener, and finish with a decision or action that can be understood. This is more useful for V8 than a general “speak confidently” prompt because it makes the listener and the next action visible.

The source also supports a retry loop. If a listener cannot identify the point, the stake, or the requested action, the speaker has a concrete reason to revise the explanation and try again. The listener can evaluate the message structure without judging accent, personality, or commercial success.

## Concrete V8 recommendation: One-Ask Brief

Create one original **One-Ask Brief** entry for an adult learner or small company group. Use this generic scenario: *Your team is considering a weekly 20-minute cross-team check-in to reduce duplicate work.* The scenario is deliberately illustrative; it does not assert that duplicate work exists in any participant’s company.

Run the activity in 8–12 minutes:

1. **Choose the listener — 1 minute.** Pick a generic listener such as a team lead, peer, or project partner. Decide what that person can approve or do next.
2. **State the idea — 2 minutes.** Complete: “I propose that we ___.” Keep it to one sentence and one action.
3. **Explain why it matters — 2 minutes.** Complete: “This matters because ___.” Give one observable example from the fictional scenario or an anonymized experience. Do not invent a metric.
4. **Make the ask — 1 minute.** Complete: “I’m asking you to ___ by ___.” If no decision is possible, ask for one specific next step, such as a short trial or a follow-up conversation.
5. **Deliver — 1 minute.** Say the idea, why it matters, and the ask in 45–60 seconds.
6. **Listener check — 2 minutes.** The listener says, “I heard your idea was ___,” then answers, “The reason it matters sounded like ___,” and asks one question: “What would you need from me next?”
7. **Retry — 1–2 minutes.** The speaker changes one unclear part and delivers a second 30–45 second version. End with: “The change I made was ___.”

On mobile, show one step per card with a visible step count, the sentence stem, the time target, and the single listener job. Keep the idea, why, ask, feedback, and retry cards short enough to scan without scrolling. Provide `Back`, `Next`, and `Try again` controls, and let a teacher or facilitator restart the round. Do not require recording, login, public sharing, chat, or a live booking.

If V8 adds a CMS entry for this activity, candidate fields are `title`, `audience`, `scenario`, `ideaPrompt`, `whyPrompt`, `askPrompt`, `listenerChecks`, `retryPrompt`, `minutes`, `teacherNote`, `reviewedOn`, and `editorialNote`. Those fields describe a proposed original practice card, not a syllabus or a corporate client deliverable.

## Verification and evidence boundary

Before release, verify that a first-time adult can identify the listener, complete the three-part explanation, understand the ask, receive the two feedback stems, and retry on a narrow mobile viewport within the stated time. Test that every prompt remains usable when the participant substitutes an anonymized scenario. Check keyboard access, visible focus, readable timer text, English/Chinese copy if the entry is bilingual, and a clear no-JavaScript limitation if the interaction depends on scripts.

Useful observations are activity starts, completed explanation rounds where instrumentation exists, retry use, listener confusion, and optional teacher feedback. These are product signals only. They do not establish that the activity improves clarity, persuasion, approvals, revenue, or workplace performance.
